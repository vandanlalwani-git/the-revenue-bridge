    How the credit-loss allowance is worked out (IFRS 9 provision matrix)

    1. Group every unpaid invoice at month-end by how many times the customer's card has been declined. Stripe retries on days 3, 7 and 14, then writes the invoice off.
    2. Apply a loss rate to each group: the share of such balances actually lost over the past 12 months. A group further along never gets a lower rate than an earlier one.
    3. Add up the expected losses. The total is the allowance. Bad-debt expense is this month's write-offs plus the change in the allowance.

    The loss rate is measured in dollars, and counts only the part of a lost invoice that had already been earned (the rest was never revenue). The count under it shows how many invoices in that group over the past year were never paid.

    Can you trust the notes?

    Manual review notes

      Method

      Every number on this page comes from SQL over Stripe-shaped data, and is rounded by one display module shared with the notes and the evaluator. The same SQL was also run, unchanged, on billing data created by Stripe itself.

      How it's builtpipeline, numbers, real-Stripe check, frozen-prompt protocol
      scenario.yaml→seeded generator→Stripe-shaped parquet→DuckDB SQL→facts files→claude -p, no tools→evaluator→this page

      The numbers

      • All money is integer cents; MRR is held as yearly cents so annual plans stay exact.
      • MRR is rebuilt from invoice lines, because Stripe's subscription table only shows today.
      • Revenue is recognised by the second over the service delivered (IFRS 15); credit losses use a provision matrix (IFRS 9).
      • Every bridge and rollforward has an Unexplained line computed in exact cents. It is $0.00 in all 30 months.

      The frozen-prompt protocol

      • The answer key was finalised after the data was generated and before any note was written; every adjustment is logged in the write-up.
      • Before the freeze, the only check was a format lint on a separate practice dataset.
      • The prompt's SHA-256, the model and the CLI version are recorded; the evaluator refuses to run on any mismatch.
      • The note writer has no tools and no file access, so it cannot see the answer key.

      Checked on real Stripe

      • 6 customers were played through 4 months on Stripe test clocks, in a Stripe test-mode account: a trial, an annual prepayment, an upgrade, extra seats, a 20% coupon, a refund, a card failure that recovered, and one where Stripe's own retries gave up and cancelled the subscription.
      • Stripe's objects were exported into the same tables and the unchanged SQL was run on them. Every reconciliation and rollforward ties to $0.00, and MRR matches the scenario worked out by hand.
      • The 30 months on this page are still the seeded synthetic data; the Stripe run shows the SQL is not fitted to it.

      What real Stripe taught us

      • A subscription coupon is recorded on the invoice, not on each line; the line only carries the discount amount.
      • Stripe retried the failed card many times over about 2 weeks, cancelled the subscription as "payment failed" and left the invoice open. Writing it off is finance's step, as it is here.
      • Test clocks move invoices and subscriptions in simulated time, but stamp charges and refunds with real time. The export puts each payment back in the month it belongs to; a live account doesn't need this.
